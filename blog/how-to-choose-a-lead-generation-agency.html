How to choose a B2B lead generation agency: 12 questions to ask

By the ClearLeads team · Updated November 2026 · 7 min read

A practical checklist for comparing agencies, and the red flags that should end the conversation.

To choose a B2B lead generation agency, judge it on how it defines and delivers qualified meetings. Ask how a qualified meeting is defined, whose domains they send from, how they protect deliverability, who handles replies, how they report, and what the contract terms are. The best agencies report weekly on meetings booked and work month-to-month after a short ramp.

Questions about results

  1. How do you define a qualified meeting? Get it in writing: title, company size, geography, and intent. Vague definitions are how agencies hit targets that don't turn into pipeline.
  2. What happens with no-shows? Good agencies replace meetings that don't happen for reasons within their control.
  3. Can I speak to a current client in my space? A reference call beats any case study PDF.
  4. What forecast would you give me, and why? A credible agency gives a range and explains its assumptions. Be wary of anyone who guarantees a precise number before seeing your offer.

Questions about how they work

  1. Whose domains do you send from? The right answer is dedicated lookalike domains, never your primary domain.
  2. How do you protect deliverability? Listen for inbox warm-up, SPF/DKIM/DMARC, low volume per inbox, and verified lists. See our deliverability guide for what good looks like.
  3. Who writes the copy, and do I approve it? You should approve messaging before anything is sent under your brand.
  4. Who handles replies? Real people qualifying and scheduling convert far more replies into meetings than auto-responders.
  5. Which channels do you run? Email plus LinkedIn usually outperforms either one alone.

Questions about the commercial terms

  1. What's the contract length? A short ramp followed by month-to-month keeps the agency accountable.
  2. What's included in the fee? Check whether domains, inboxes, data, and tools are included or billed on top.
  3. What do I own if we stop? Ask about the lead lists, the copy, and the domains when the engagement ends.

Red flags

  • Guaranteed meeting numbers before they understand your offer.
  • Reporting on opens and "leads" instead of qualified meetings.
  • Sending from your main domain.
  • Twelve-month lock-ins with no performance exit.
  • No access to the raw activity: sends, replies, and meetings.
Shortcut: the best agencies are measured on qualified meetings booked, report weekly on real numbers, and let you leave if they don't deliver.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What should I look for in a lead generation agency?

Look for a written definition of a qualified meeting, dedicated sending domains and proven deliverability practice, human reply handling, multi-channel outreach, transparent weekly reporting, and short or month-to-month contracts.

Should a lead generation agency guarantee results?

Be cautious of precise guarantees made before the agency understands your offer and market. A credible agency gives a realistic forecast range, explains its assumptions, and replaces no-show or unqualified meetings.

How long should a lead generation contract be?

A typical healthy structure is a short initial ramp of one to three months followed by month-to-month. Long lock-ins remove the agency's incentive to keep performing.
